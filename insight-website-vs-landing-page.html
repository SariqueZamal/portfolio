Website vs Landing Page: Which Does Your Business Need?

By Sarique Zamal • 2026

If you're planning to grow your business online, you may be wondering whether you need a complete website or just a landing page.

Both can help your business attract customers, generate enquiries, and increase sales. However, they serve different purposes.

A business website helps people understand your company, explore your services, and build trust in your brand. A landing page focuses on a specific offer and encourages visitors to take one particular action.

The right choice depends on your business goals, marketing strategy, and how you want to attract customers.

In this guide, we'll explain the difference between a website and a landing page, their benefits, and how to choose the right solution for your business.

1. What Is a Business Website?

A business website is your company's online home. It provides information about your business, services, expertise, and contact details in one place.

A typical business website may include a homepage, About page, service pages, portfolio, blog, and contact page.

For example, a digital marketing agency can use its website to explain its services, showcase previous projects, publish helpful articles, and encourage potential clients to request a consultation.

Benefits of a business website

  • Builds credibility: Presents your business professionally and helps potential customers evaluate your services.
  • Improves online visibility: Gives search engines relevant pages to discover and potentially rank.
  • Showcases your services: Allows you to explain different offerings in detail.
  • Supports long-term marketing: Provides a destination for traffic from Google, social media, referrals, and advertising.
  • Grows with your business: You can add new services, case studies, blog posts, and integrations over time.
  • Supports automation: Can connect to CRM platforms, booking systems, enquiry forms, and AI-powered workflows.

A business website is particularly useful when you want to establish a strong online presence and attract customers through multiple channels.

2. What Is a Landing Page?

A landing page is a standalone web page designed around a specific marketing goal.

Unlike a typical business website, which provides information about several aspects of a company, a landing page concentrates on one offer, audience, or conversion objective.

For example, a fitness studio running an advertisement for a free trial could direct visitors to a landing page explaining the offer and providing a form to book a session.

The page would focus on the offer rather than asking visitors to explore multiple services.

Benefits of a landing page

  • Focuses attention: Keeps visitors centred on one offer or objective.
  • Supports advertising campaigns: Provides a relevant destination for Google Ads, social media ads, or email campaigns.
  • Encourages action: Uses a clear call to action, such as booking a consultation or requesting a quote.
  • Simplifies lead collection: Can capture contact details through a dedicated enquiry form.
  • Makes campaign testing easier: Allows businesses to test different headlines, offers, and page layouts.
  • Measures campaign performance: Helps track conversions associated with a specific campaign.

A landing page is especially useful when you have a clear offer and want visitors to take a specific action.

3. Website vs Landing Page: What's the Difference?

Feature Business website Landing page
Main purpose Present your business and services Encourage a specific action
Structure Multiple pages Usually one focused page
Content Covers several business topics Focuses on one offer or objective
SEO opportunities Supports multiple search topics and service pages Can target a specific search intent or offer
Advertising Can support campaigns Often tailored to a particular campaign
Customer journey Explore, compare, and learn Take a focused next step
Best suited for Long-term online presence Campaigns and specific conversions
Future expansion Can grow into a broader digital platform Can be created for individual offers

Neither option is automatically better. The right choice depends on what you need your online presence to achieve.

4. When Does Your Business Need a Website?

A business website is generally the better starting point if you want to establish your brand and build a lasting online presence.

Consider investing in a website if:

You're starting a new business. You need a central place to explain what you do, introduce your business, and provide contact information.

You offer multiple services. Dedicated service pages help potential customers understand your different offerings.

You want to attract customers through Google. A well-structured website gives you opportunities to publish useful content and target relevant search queries over time.

Your customers need information before buying. Detailed service descriptions, FAQs, portfolios, and genuine testimonials can help them make informed decisions.

You want to grow your business over time. A website provides a foundation for future marketing, content, e-commerce, and automation initiatives.

For many service-based businesses, a professional website is the most practical long-term starting point.

5. When Does Your Business Need a Landing Page?

A landing page may be the better choice when your marketing objective is narrow and clearly defined.

You may benefit from a landing page if:

You're running paid advertising. Direct visitors to a page that matches the message and offer in your advertisement.

You're promoting one specific service. Focus on the benefits of that service and encourage visitors to request a consultation.

You're launching a product or offer. Explain the value proposition, address objections, and guide visitors towards purchasing or registering.

You want to generate leads. Use a focused page with an enquiry form and a clear next step.

You're testing a new offer. Evaluate whether a particular message or proposition generates interest before investing in a larger campaign.

For example, a real estate agency could have a full website showcasing its business and properties, alongside a separate landing page promoting a particular residential project.

The website establishes the brand. The landing page supports the campaign.

6. Which Is Better for SEO: A Website or a Landing Page?

If your primary goal is long-term organic visibility, a well-structured business website usually offers more flexibility.

You can create individual pages for different services, answer customer questions through blog articles, and develop content around relevant search topics.

A landing page can also appear in search results if it provides useful, relevant content and meets search engine requirements. However, a single page generally covers fewer topics than a comprehensive website.

For example, a website development agency could create separate pages for business website development, e-commerce development, and AI automation services. Each page can address a different customer need.

The key is to create genuinely useful content rather than publishing pages solely to target keywords.

Our recommendation: Build a website for your long-term SEO strategy and use dedicated landing pages when you need to support specific offers or marketing campaigns.

7. Can You Have Both a Website and a Landing Page?

Absolutely. In many cases, combining both is the most effective approach.

Your website establishes your business identity, explains your services, and supports organic discovery. Your landing pages focus on individual campaigns and conversion goals.

Imagine a business offering website development services.

Its main website could contain:

  • A homepage introducing the agency.
  • A website development service page.
  • An AI automation service page.
  • A portfolio and case studies.
  • A blog with helpful business articles.
  • A contact page.

The agency could also create a dedicated landing page for a campaign offering website consultations to small businesses.

Visitors from that campaign would see a focused message, relevant benefits, and a clear consultation form.

This approach lets your business build long-term credibility while supporting specific lead-generation activities.

8. Website vs Landing Page: What Should a Small Business Choose First?

If you're a small business owner working with a limited budget, start by identifying your immediate priority.

Choose a website first if:

  • You don't yet have a professional online presence.
  • You need to explain multiple services.
  • You want to build credibility and support organic search visibility.
  • You want a digital foundation that can grow with your business.

Choose a landing page first if:

  • You already have an established online presence.
  • You have one specific offer to promote.
  • You're running a focused advertising campaign.
  • Your immediate objective is to generate enquiries for one service.

Choose both if:

  • You want a long-term online presence and focused lead-generation campaigns.
  • You have multiple services and different customer segments.
  • You want to connect website enquiries with CRM systems and automated follow-ups.

You don't need to build everything at once. Start with the solution that addresses your most important business need, then expand when the benefits justify the investment.
